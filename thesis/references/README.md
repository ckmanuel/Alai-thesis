## Theme 1 – Call-to-action phrases as persuasive linguistic units

### Local copies now in this folder

1. **Arakelyan, H., & Aleksanyan, A. (2025).** Linguistic peculiarities of call-to-action phrases in digital discourse. *Foreign Languages in Higher Education, 29*(2).  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.46991/FLHE.2025.29.2.005  
   File: `Arakelyan_Aleksanyan_2025_Linguistic_Peculiarities_CTA.pdf`  
   Direct linguistic analysis of CTA phrases (action verbs, urgency, concise structure, multimodality).

2. **Nguyen, D. S. (2025).** Interpreting creative call to action messages in advertising discourse using requestive strategies. *Journal of Media & Management, 7*(4).  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.47363/JMM/2025(7)190  
   File: `Nguyen_2025_Creative_CTA_Requestive_Strategies.pdf`  
   Speech-act / requestive strategies (direct vs indirect) applied to CTA messages.

3. **De Cock, B., Aulit, L., Cigada, S., Greco, S., Modrzejewska, E., & Palmieri, R. (2024).** The discourse of digital activism: A linguistic analysis of calls for action concerning the Fashion Revolution. *Applied Linguistics, 45*(6), 1091–1110.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amae046  
   File: `DeCock_etal_2024_Discourse_Digital_Activism.docx`  
   Linguistic analysis of deontic structures and imperatives in calls for action on social media.

4. **Szabó, Á. K., & Mitev, A. Z. (2025).** When online advertising backfires: How imperative call-to-action messages trigger psychological reactance. *Journal of Research in Interactive Marketing*.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1108/JRIM-09-2024-0422  
   File: `Szabo_Mitev_2025_Imperative_CTA_Reactance.pdf`  
   Experimental evidence that strong imperative CTAs can provoke reactance and reduce effectiveness on social media.

5. **Bünzli, F., & Eppler, M. J. (2025).** Calls to action and user engagement: The role of visual and verbal requests in nonprofit advocacy on Facebook. *Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 18*, Article 100611.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100611  
   File: `Bunzli_Eppler_2025_CTA_User_Engagement_Facebook.pdf`  
   Open-access content analysis of 2,250 Facebook posts; examines direct vs indirect verbal requests and visual (gaze) requests.
