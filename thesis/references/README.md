## Theme 1 (current numbering) – Facebook / social-media advertising language as a short promotional genre

### Local copies now in this folder

1. **Ul Islam, Z. (2025).** *Social media persuasion in tourism: Linguistic strategies in Facebook and Instagram tourism advertisements – A critical discourse analysis* [Term paper / SSRN preprint]. Higher School of Economics.  
   SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6037054  
   File: `UlIslam_2025_Social_Media_Persuasion_Tourism_Facebook_Instagram.pdf`  
   Facebook tourism ads: transactional style, imperatives, urgency lexicon, direct CTAs (vs Instagram aspirational). **Domain caveat:** tourism, not skincare. Preprint / term paper.

2. **Al-Subhi, A. S. (2022).** Metadiscourse in online advertising: Exploring linguistic and visual metadiscourse in social media advertisements. *Journal of Pragmatics, 187*, 24–40.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2021.10.027  
   File: `Al-Subhi_2022_Metadiscourse_Online_Advertising_Social_Media.pdf`  
   Engagement markers and directives high in SM ads. **Platform caveat:** Instagram, Snapchat, Twitter (not Facebook). Many ads from cosmetic/beauty brands.

3. **Bünzli, F., & Eppler, M. J. (2025).** Calls to action and user engagement: The role of visual and verbal requests in nonprofit advocacy on Facebook. *Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 18*, Article 100611.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100611  
   File: `Bunzli_Eppler_2025_CTA_User_Engagement_Facebook.pdf`  
   Already listed under older Theme 1 block; Facebook verbal/visual requests.

4. **Septianasari, L., Baihaqi, Y., Abqoriyyah, F. H., & Syaputri, W. (2021).** Language of persuasion in online marketing of cosmetic products: A glance of netnography in pragmatics. *PRASASTI: Journal of Linguistics, 6*(1), 74–83.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.20961/prasasti.v6i1.44323  
   File: `Septianasari_etal_2021_Language_Persuasion_Online_Marketing_Cosmetic.pdf`  
   Directives dominate cosmetics sellers on Facebook and Instagram.

## Theme 3 – Rhetorical appeals in advertising discourse

### Local copies now in this folder

1. **De Castro, G. L., & Martinez, S. P. (2025).** Rhetorical appeals across selected Philippine bank advertising on Facebook. *Southern Semiotic Review*, (22), 243–262.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.33234/SSR.22.13  
   Open PDF: http://www.southernsemioticreview.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/de-Castro_SSR-22-2025-ii_13-Google-Docs.pdf  
   File: `DeCastro_Martinez_2025_Rhetorical_Appeals_Philippine_Bank_Facebook.pdf`  
   PH Facebook picture ads (banks); ethos+logos combination most frequent.

2. **Dwiyanti, W. G. (2024).** Persuasive techniques in makeup and skincare video advertisements. *JLT Jurnal Linguistik Terapan, 14*(1), 27–37.  
   https://jurnal.polinema.ac.id/index.php/jlt/article/view/4558  
   Open PDF: https://jurnal.polinema.ac.id/index.php/jlt/article/download/4558/4192  
   File: `Dwiyanti_2024_Persuasive_Techniques_Makeup_Skincare_Video.pdf`  
   Makeup/skincare video ads; pathos most frequent among ethos/pathos/logos.

3. **Firdous, T., Li, H. F., & Saleh, A.-N. Y. A. (2023).** The Aristotelian rhetorical theory: A framework for analyzing Pakistani beauty products advertising. *Indian Journal of Mass Communication and Journalism, 2*(3), 1–7.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.54105/ijmcj.C1028.032323  
   Open PDF: https://www.ijmcj.latticescipub.com/wp-content/uploads/papers/v2i3/C1028032323.pdf  
   File: `Firdous_Li_Saleh_2023_Aristotelian_Rhetoric_Pakistani_Beauty.pdf`  
   Pakistani beauty TV ads; all three appeals used.

4. **Saad, I. T., & Ismael, A. A. (2025).** The art of selling beauty: Analyzing persuasive strategies and speech acts in social media cosmetics advertising.  
   File: `Saad_Ismael_2025_Persuasive_Strategies_Social_Media_Cosmetics.pdf`  
   Cosmetics on Instagram/Facebook; ethos, pathos, logos + speech acts (bridges Theme 3 and joint gap).

5. **Talaue, G. M. (2022).** Aristotle’s rhetorical triangle as applied to advertisements: Its impact to Saudi Arabian college student consumers. *International Journal of Applied Management Theory and Research, 4*(1), 1–18.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.4018/IJAMTR.300280  
   File: `Talaue_2022_Aristotle_Rhetorical_Triangle_Saudi_Ads.pdf`  
   Consumer preference study; use lightly only (audience/culture can shift appeal ranking). Not a linguistic coding of ad text.

**Method-fit note:** Theme 3 thickens with sources that **code or describe appeals in ad language** (beauty and/or PH/digital). Avoid engagement ROI or pure consumer-impact papers as primary pillars.


## Theme 4 – Directive speech acts in promotional language

### Local copies now in this folder

1. **Anggraini, M., & Afriana, A. (2023).** Types of directive speech acts in “Fall” movie 2022. *IDEAS: Journal on English Language Teaching and Learning, Linguistics and Literature, 11*(2), 1132–1143.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.24256/ideas.v11i2.4250  
   File: `Anggraini_Afriana_2023_Types_Directive_Speech_Acts_Fall_Movie.pdf`  
   Applies Yule (1996) subtypes (command, order, request, suggestion) to movie data.

2. **Kencana, D. S. S. (2025).** An analysis of representative and directive speech acts in NIVEA’s advertising discourse on Instagram. *PRASASTI: Journal of Linguistics, 10*(2), 184–197.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.20961/prasasti.v10i2.97398  
   File: `Kencana_2025_Representative_Directive_Speech_Acts_NIVEA_Instagram.pdf`  
   NIVEA Indonesia Instagram captions; directives as calls to action (commands, suggestions, invitations, advice).

3. **Nurjannah. (2025).** Analysis of directive utterances in “Some by Me” advertisement in Instagram. *Social Science Research Journal, 2*(2), 56–63.  
   File: `Nurjannah_2025_Directive_Utterances_Some_By_Me_Instagram.pdf`  
   Skincare brand Instagram; predominantly imperatives (direct and indirect); modals and rhetorical questions for politeness.

4. **Mahdiati, M., Azizah, H. N., Hadi, M. I., & Jumadi, J. (2025).** Tindak tutur direktif dalam pembentukan citra produk pada digital kosmetik di Instagram @wardahbeauty. *Argopuro: Jurnal Ilmu Bahasa, 9*(4).  
   File: `Mahdiati_etal_2025_Directive_Speech_Acts_Wardah_Instagram.pdf`  
   Wardah Beauty Instagram; suggestions, commands, and invitations used to shape product image.

5. **Andriuzzi, A., & Michel, G. (2025).** Embarrassed to observe: The effects of directive language in brand conversation. *Psychology & Marketing, 42*(11), 2922–2938.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.70018  
   File: `Andriuzzi_Michel_2025_Effects_Directive_Language_Brand_Conversation.pdf`  
   Directive language in brand–consumer social-media conversation can reduce observer engagement via vicarious embarrassment.

6. **Searle, J. R. (1975).** A taxonomy of illocutionary acts. In K. Gunderson (Ed.), *Language, mind, and knowledge* (Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science, Vol. 7, pp. 344–369). University of Minnesota Press.  
   File: `Searle_1975_Taxonomy_Illocutionary_Acts.pdf`  
   Classic taxonomy; directives as attempts to get the hearer to do something.

7. **Yule, G. (1996).** *Pragmatics*. Oxford University Press.  
   ISBN: 978-0-19-437207-7  
   File: `Yule_1996_Pragmatics.pdf`  
   Source for the four directive subtypes (command, order, request, suggestion) used operationally in the thesis.
